# The Causes of Climate Change

Human activities are driving the global warming trend observed since the mid-20th century. The greenhouse effect is essential to life on Earth, but human-made emissions in the atmosphere are trapping and slowing heat loss to space. While the Sun has played a role in past climate changes, the evidence shows the current warming cannot be explained by the Sun.

## Increasing Greenhouse Gases Are Warming the Planet

Scientists attribute the global warming trend observed since the mid-20th century to the human expansion of the greenhouse effect: warming that results when the atmosphere traps heat radiating from Earth toward space.

Life on Earth depends on energy coming from the Sun. About half the light energy reaching Earth's atmosphere passes through the air and clouds to the surface, where it is absorbed and radiated in the form of infrared heat. About 90% of this heat is then absorbed by greenhouse gases and re-radiated, slowing heat loss to space.

## Four Major Gases That Contribute to the Greenhouse Effect

Carbon dioxide is a vital component of the atmosphere. It is released through natural processes, like volcanic eruptions, and through human activities, such as burning fossil fuels and deforestation.

Methane comes from both natural and human-caused sources. It comes from plant-matter breakdown in wetlands and is also released from landfills and rice farming. Livestock emit methane from their digestion and manure, and leaks from fossil fuel production and transportation are another major source. Natural gas is 70% to 90% methane.

Nitrous oxide is a potent greenhouse gas produced by farming practices. It is released during commercial and organic fertilizer production and use, and it also comes from burning fossil fuels and vegetation. It has increased by 18% in the last 100 years.

Chlorofluorocarbons do not exist in nature; they are entirely of industrial origin. They were used as refrigerants, solvents, and spray can propellants.

## Water Vapor Is a Feedback, Not a Cause

Water vapor is the most abundant greenhouse gas. But because the warming ocean increases the amount of it in our atmosphere, it is not a direct cause of climate change. It is a feedback: as Earth warms, the air holds more water vapor, and that leads to even more warming.

A forcing is different. A forcing is something acting upon Earth's climate that changes how energy flows through it, such as long-lasting, heat-trapping gases. These gases slow outgoing heat in the atmosphere and cause the planet to warm.

## Human Activity Is the Cause of Increased Greenhouse Gas Concentrations

Over the last century, burning fossil fuels like coal and oil has increased the concentration of atmospheric carbon dioxide. Burning combines carbon with oxygen in the air to make carbon dioxide. To a lesser extent, clearing land for agriculture, industry, and other human activities has also increased concentrations of greenhouse gases.

The industrial activities that our modern civilization depends upon have raised atmospheric carbon dioxide levels by nearly 50% since 1750. Scientists know this increase is due to human activities because they can see a distinctive isotopic fingerprint in the atmosphere.

In its Sixth Assessment Report, the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change concluded that it is unequivocal that the increase of carbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide over the industrial era is the result of human activities. As the panel put it: "Since systematic scientific assessments began in the 1970s, the influence of human activity on the warming of the climate system has evolved from theory to established fact."

## The Sun Cannot Explain Current Warming

Scientists use a metric called Total Solar Irradiance to measure changes in the energy Earth receives from the Sun. It incorporates the 11-year solar cycle and solar flares and storms from the Sun's surface.

Solar variability has played a role in past climate changes. A decrease in solar activity, coupled with increased volcanic activity, helped trigger the Little Ice Age. But the amount of solar energy Earth receives has followed the Sun's natural 11-year cycle of small ups and downs with no net increase since 1880. Over the same period, global temperature has risen markedly.

Several lines of evidence show that current warming cannot be explained by changes in energy from the Sun:

- Since 1750, the average amount of energy from the Sun has either remained constant or decreased slightly.
- If a more active Sun caused the warming, scientists would expect warmer temperatures in all layers of the atmosphere. Instead, they observe cooling in the upper atmosphere and warming at the surface, because greenhouse gases slow heat loss from the lower atmosphere.
- Climate models that include solar irradiance changes cannot reproduce the observed temperature trend over the past century without including a rise in greenhouse gases.
